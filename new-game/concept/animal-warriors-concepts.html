Chapter 1 · Dust Pit

Team Battle

Players collect warriors, build a team of 3 to 5, and send them through chapters of fights. Battles run automatically; the player's skill is in team choice, upgrades, and timing each warrior's spell. Between fights: unlock new warriors, level them up, and dress them in the Scout or Heavy Armor outfits from your sheets.

Why it fits

  • Uses the whole roster. 55 now and 110 later means years of "new warrior" updates.
  • One-thumb play suits casual players.
  • Natural rewarded ads: double rewards, revive, free chest.

Watch out for

  • Needs careful balancing as warriors are added.
  • Can feel passive if spells don't matter. Spell timing fixes that.

My pick. It shows off the most warriors with the least animation, and it's the easiest to grow after launch.

Arena Duel

One warrior against one rival, in real time. Strike, block when the rival winds up, and save mana for a big spell. A season ladder of rivals, with your warrior getting stronger between fights.

Why it fits

  • The most exciting moment-to-moment play.
  • Shows your art big on screen.
  • 12 warriors is plenty for a launch.

Watch out for

  • A good fighter needs real animation for every warrior: idle, attack, hit, block. That's the most art work by far.
  • Fewer natural ad moments.

The most fun to watch, and the most expensive to make well. Good as a second game or a mode inside Team Battle later.

Tactics

Turn-based battles on a grid, like chess with abilities. Move each warrior, then strike. Weapons matter: spears hit from 2 tiles away, the Tortoise is slow but hard to kill, the Gecko moves far. Maps add rocks, rivers and high ground.

Why it fits

  • Hardly any animation: warriors slide and flash.
  • Weapon types finally matter, which suits your designs.
  • Ads fit as "undo a move" or "extra reinforcement".

Watch out for

  • Slower, thinking game. A smaller audience than quick-play games.
  • Small tokens hide your detailed art.

The cheapest to build well, but your art is mostly seen as small tokens.

Card Battle

Every warrior is a card. Spend mana to put warriors into three lanes; at the end of your turn each one attacks whatever is across from it, or the rival chief if the lane is empty. Potions and spellbooks from your sheets become spell cards.

Why it fits

  • The reference sheets become card art straight away.
  • Collecting 110 cards is a strong long-term hook.
  • Easy ad rewards: free card pack, extra draw.

Watch out for

  • Card balance is fiddly.
  • Rules take a little teaching, so the tutorial matters.

A strong option if you like strategy games. Second place for me.
